# Page copy standards

## Who the page speaks to

- Address the reader as you, and say what they do or get. A page about the product reads as a manual to someone deciding whether to use it.
- Keep the reader as the subject rather than the product or the team. Use we only where the reader needs to know who acts.

## How a sentence opens

- Open a sentence on a verb or on the reader when it says what the reader does or gets, as in "Bring everything into one system of record".
- Lead with what the reader gets, then how it works. A reader who stops after one line should still know why to stay.
- Keep a sentence that opens on the thing itself for a fact the reader came to look up, such as a limit or a supported version.

## Headline and supporting line

- Make the page's first headline say what the product does for the reader, in words a competitor could not put on their own page.
- Write a headline that makes sense on its own, with its key words first, since a reader often reads only the first few.
- Start a section heading on a verb when the section is something the reader does, such as `Plan a feature`.
- Let the supporting line add what the headline left out, such as how or for whom, and repeat none of its words.

## One idea per block

- Give each block one idea, and open the block on it.
- Move a second idea to its own block, or cut it.

## Where it applies

- Apply these rules to the headlines, supporting lines, and section copy of a page a stranger reads to decide, such as a landing or pricing page. Long-form body text stays with `write-human`.
- Keep `copy.md` in force on page copy, since a headline is still a rendered heading.
- Keep `write-human`'s voice and word choice in force on page copy, since this reference adds who it speaks to and how it opens, never a word list.
