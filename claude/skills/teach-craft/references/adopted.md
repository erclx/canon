# Adopted and declined

The field is learning science and cognitive psychology, with educational measurement for quiz construction. Every source below was opened, the papers through the PubMed API and the practice guide as a PDF. Each entry quotes the passage relied on, and a later session extends this file rather than re-arguing an item settled here.

A source a bullet rests on is linked once, in that bullet's row of the toolkit's per-bullet ledger at `governance/ledger/skills/teach-craft/`, which ships outside the plugin. This file names those sources without the link and keeps the catalog-level adopted and declined record, linking only a source no row cites.

## Adopted

### Retrieval over restudy, and fluency against retention

Roediger and Karpicke 2006, Test-enhanced learning backs testing before reviewing, and the rule that confidence says nothing about retention.

- Passage: "prior testing produced substantially greater retention than studying, even though repeated studying increased students' confidence in their ability to remember the material"
- The same abstract: "When the final test was given after 5 min, repeated studying improved recall relative to repeated testing"
- Adopted in `pedagogy.md` as the fluency and retrieval sections, and in the body's excuses table

### Failing an attempt before the answer

Kornell, Hays and Bjork 2009, Unsuccessful retrieval attempts enhance subsequent learning backs asking before telling and letting the learner fail first.

- Passage: in the test condition "participants attempted to answer the question before being shown the answer", and "Unsuccessful retrieval attempts enhanced learning with both types of materials"
- The read-only condition it beat is "the question and answer were presented together"
- Adopted as the rule to open on the question, and in `lesson-craft.md` as feedback after the attempt rather than beside the question

### Spacing, and the gap growing with the interval

[Cepeda et al. 2006, Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/) backs spacing, and the best gap growing with how long the material has to last.

- Passage: "the ISI producing maximal retention increased as retention interval increased"
- Adopted as the spacing section's principle. It names no schedule and reports no result for expanding gaps, so the ladder is house judgment.

Pashler et al. 2007, IES practice guide NCER 2007-2004 backs the same at classroom scale, and its panel rates the evidence moderate.

- Passage: key concepts seen on at least two occasions, "separated by a period of several weeks to several months"
- Adopted as the rule to revisit once forgetting has begun rather than while the material is fresh

### Quizzing at every phase

The same IES guide, in Recommendation 5, backs carrying a quiz in every lesson. Its panel rates quizzes that re-expose key content as strong evidence and pre-questions as low.

- Passage: "Use quizzing with active retrieval of information at all phases of the learning process"
- Adopted as the rule to carry a quiz. Opening on a question rests on Kornell, Hays and Bjork above, with this as weak support.

### Worked examples while acquiring

The IES guide's Recommendation 2 backs worked examples for a learner taking on a procedure, rated moderate.

- Passage: students "learn more by alternating between studying examples of worked-out problem solutions and solving similar problems on their own"
- Adopted as the clause introducing new material through worked examples. The wider split between skill and knowledge is house judgment below.

### Interleaving exemplars

Kornell and Bjork 2008, Learning concepts and categories backs mixing related items within a session, and names the fluency trap of blocked practice.

- Passage: paintings "presented consecutively (massed) or interleaved with other artists' paintings (spaced)", and induction profited from the interleaved order
- The same abstract: "Participants rated massing as more effective than spacing"
- Adopted as the claim that blocked practice feels better and teaches less. The study mixes exemplars of categories, so mixing topics is an extension, recorded below.

### Feedback on a quiz

Butler and Roediger 2008, Feedback and multiple-choice testing backs giving feedback on a multiple-choice quiz.

- Passage: "The selection of lures can lead students to acquire false knowledge", and feedback "reduced the proportion of intrusions"
- Adopted as the rule to write feedback for every question. It tests feedback against none, not where distractors come from or how feedback explains them.
- It found immediate and delayed feedback both help, so the timing rule rests on Kornell, Hays and Bjork instead

### The teach-back

Nestojko et al. 2014, Expecting to teach backs asking the learner to explain to an audience.

- Passage: "Participants expecting to teach produced more complete and better organized free recall of the passage"
- Adopted as the teach-back block and its named audience
- The participants expected to teach and never taught. The finding backs framing the ask as teaching, not a gain from the explanation itself.

## Declined

### A fixed schedule from the literature

Cepeda's synthesis and the IES guide each tie the best gap to how long the material has to last, and neither names a sequence of days. Neither is cited for the ladder, so the ladder does not read as sourced.

### Dunlosky et al. 2013

[Improving students' learning](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266) refused with a 403. It stays a lead, never cited, and is the first source to open when extending the retrieval or spacing rules.

### Leads not opened

Bjork on desirable difficulties, Vygotsky on the zone of proximal development, and Haladyna, Downing and Rodriguez 2002 on item writing were each searched for and not opened. The item-writing review refused with a 403 at the publisher, and Crossref carries no abstract for it. Each rule it would back is labeled below.

## House judgment

These rules stay with no opened source behind them, each for the reason given.

- **The review ladder.** The 1, 3, 7, 16, and 35 day rungs, and moving a rung on each hit or miss, are a house schedule. An author told to widen a gap settles on whatever feels right.
- **Forgetting as the mechanism of spacing.** It explains why the gap matters, and no opened source tests it.
- **Production over selection, and no cue in the question.** Both studies above compare testing with restudy, and neither compares a recognition test.
- **Misconception distractors, explained one by one.** A distractor nobody holds removes itself, and Butler tests only feedback against none.
- **Interleaving topics rather than exemplars.** This extends Kornell and Bjork and the IES worked-example finding. It stays because working out which approach a problem calls for carries over.
- **Never interleaving an unseen topic.** Telling two things apart needs something on each side.
- **Where difficulty helps and hurts.** The split stays because it changes what a lesson withholds. Kornell, Hays and Bjork back difficulty at retrieval, and IES backs worked examples while acquiring.
- **The zone of proximal development.** Placing the learner just past what they can do unaided is how a session picks the next step from the learning record.
- **Equal option length.** A longer option reads as the considered one, and a session can check the rule by counting.
- **Rotating the correct slot.** An author varying position by judgment settles on the first slot.
- **Restraint and what makes a lesson worth returning to.** Both are editorial judgment about a page rather than claims about memory.
- **The sourcing ladder.** It is this toolkit's rule for what a lesson cites, not a finding about learning.
