# Accessibility standards

## Semantics

- Use a native `<button>` or `<a>` over a `<div>` or `<span>` carrying a click handler, since the native element brings its role, focus, and keyboard activation.
- Spell an attribute the way the file's syntax spells it, being `onclick` and `tabindex` in HTML and Astro markup and `onClick` and `tabIndex` in JSX.
- Give a custom control the role its pattern names, such as `combobox` or `listbox`, and keep `aria-expanded`, `aria-selected`, and `aria-checked` in step with what it shows.
- Give an interactive element with no visible text label a name through `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.
- Put the visible text in the accessible name, first where you can (WCAG 2.5.3).

## Text alternatives

- Give every `<img>` an `alt`, and use `alt=""` for a decorative image.
- Describe what an image means, or the action an icon control takes, never its appearance. Do not prefix it with "Image of" or "Photo of".

## Contrast and color

- Give a control's visual boundary and every graphic that carries meaning at least 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors (WCAG 1.4.11).
- Never make color the only carrier of a state, error, or distinction. Pair it with text, an icon, or a pattern (WCAG 1.4.1).
- Give text 4.5:1 contrast, or 3:1 when large (WCAG 1.4.3).

## Reflow

- Let content reflow at 320 CSS pixels wide with no loss and no two-dimensional scrolling, except a data table, map, or diagram (WCAG 1.4.10).
- Treat 320 as a 1280 pixel window at 400 percent zoom rather than a device width, so a layout breaking there breaks for a person who zooms.
- Hold the same 320 floor for the content region beside persistent chrome a person can resize. This extends the criterion, which measures the viewport alone.
