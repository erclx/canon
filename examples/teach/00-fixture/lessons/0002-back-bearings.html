Back bearings

A back bearing turns a forward bearing into the direction you would walk to return, which is the check that catches a bearing read backward before it costs you the walk out.

Add 180 degrees to a bearing under 180, or subtract 180 from one at or above it. Either arithmetic lands on the same reading, so pick the one that keeps the result inside 0 to 360.

  1. Point
  2. Rotate
  3. Read

Retrieval check

1. A forward bearing reads 60 degrees. What is its back bearing?

Correct: 240 degrees. 60 is under 180, so the back bearing adds 180. 120 halves the reading instead of offsetting it.
