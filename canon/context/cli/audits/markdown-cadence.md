# Markdown cadence

The reported defect was fragments, verbless clauses, uniform sentence length, and no rhythm, and no word can be banned that catches any of it. A ban list expresses negatives and every one of those symptoms is the absence of something, so the arithmetic rules the obvious fix out before it is tried: thirteen words against a vocabulary of thousands, each addition buying a false-positive class, and `just`, `allows`, and `very` carrying honest uses no literal match separates.

The measure follows the existing weights rather than replacing them. Bullet, paragraph, and depth weight already report without gating on the stated ground that they are judgments a reader settles, and a distribution belongs on the reporting side by the same test. It sits one level below where the shape layer stopped, which is the paragraph split into the sentences inside it.

Both numbers are read off the `## Rhythm` section of the `write-human` skill rather than chosen here. That skill states what good rhythm is and this measures against the statement, which is why the two shipped in this order: building the measure first would have set thresholds with nothing behind them. A spread of five words or under is one cadence and a word opening more than two sentences is a pattern, both stated there in those words.

Two of that section's rules are deliberately unimplemented and the reason is the same for each. A sentence's grammatical shape and whether it carries a finite verb each need a parse rather than a match, and a regex approximating either reads an imperative as a defect. The verbless share is the measure closest to the reported symptom, which is the argument against shipping it rather than for it, since a wrong number on the exact failure named would discredit the two that hold.

The deferral named its own release condition, being something that identifies a finite verb rather than guessing at one, and two parsers have now been measured against it over 11,389 paragraph sentences across 503 markdown files. `compromise` reports 2 percent and `wink-pos-tagger` reports 9, which is a factor of four on a number one of them must be wrong about. The first tags a fronted past participle as finite, so all twelve of those sentences in the architecture record read as carrying a verb, eleven opening `Measured at` and one `Overturned by`, and it splits on the preposition alone: `Measured at` tags a verb where `Measured against` tags an adjective. The second fixes that class and is wrong on roughly three of every four it flags, in two shapes that are not tuning problems. Hand-classifying every 21st of its 1,071 flagged sentences put 12 of 51 genuinely verbless, and the other 39 split almost evenly: nineteen imperatives, whose verb tags as a proper noun as often as a verb, and twenty ordinary declaratives whose noun-ambiguous predicate tags as a noun outright, so `Each maps to a skill.` and `Nothing checks either one.` both report verbless. This corpus runs on exactly those words: reports, answers, checks, maps, holds, names, carries, records, measures.

Telling them apart means knowing which token is the predicate, which is syntax rather than a tag. Neither tagger carries one, and nothing was found worth taking for a number nothing gates on. Three of the four shapes that would inflate the count never reach the measure anyway, since `paragraphBlocks` ends a paragraph on a heading, a list item, and a table row, so that exclusion is structural and needs no grammar. The imperative is the one sitting inside paragraph prose, and it is the one neither parser could separate. The full record sits on `measureCadence` in `src/markdown/structure.ts`, beside the code it governs.

The floor is the cheap form of an exemption rather than the exemption itself. A paragraph under three sentences is skipped, which answers the two-sentence configuration note that scored a spread of 2 on the writing-rules arm, where a rule written for prose was biting on a shape it should not govern. A shape-aware exemption, reading what a block is rather than how many sentences it holds, waits on a second case.

Neither number gates, and the claim here is weaker than the one the weight checkpoints make. Those are one judgment applied to one corpus. A healthy cadence range moves with the surface, since a catalog entry is several short sentences carrying one fact each and a page arguing a decision is not, so a single range would report the surfaces that are correct. The run states where the numbers came from beside them, which is what lets a reader read a flat number as correct for their surface.

The unit is the paragraph and the file names its worst on each measure, which follows the depth check rather than setting a precedent. A worst is named only when it crosses its checkpoint, since a healthy file offering its least healthy paragraph reads as a finding whatever the step is called.

`splitSentences` replaced the match count `countSentences` held, and the count now derives from it so the two cannot disagree. It drops a trailing span no punctuation closes, which keeps the derivation exact: the boundary pattern already matches a paragraph's final period through its end-of-text alternative, so the only span dropped is a paragraph genuinely ending without terminal punctuation, which is a fragment to both measures. `paragraphBlocks` came out of `heavyParagraphs` for the same reason, since two walks deciding what a paragraph is would drift.

Cadence counts the visible text where `heavyParagraphs` counts sentences off the raw line, and the two agree on where sentences end regardless. The boundary pattern requires whitespace after the terminal punctuation and neither a link destination nor an autolink carries any, so masking moves the word counts and never the boundaries.

Measured at `6c273324`, 2119 paragraphs reached the floor, 165 sat at or under the spread checkpoint and 50 past the opener checkpoint. Across the 60 files carrying at least ten measured paragraphs the flat rate runs zero to 21 percent against a median near 6, which is the spread that makes the number worth printing.

`BASELINE` carries that reading into the legend rather than leaving it here. It sits apart from `CHECKPOINTS` because nothing measures against it and no exit code reads it, and this entry is toolkit-internal, so a reader running the command elsewhere would otherwise get two counts and no range to place them in. The run prints its own rate beside the shipped one, which is what makes twelve flat paragraphs out of forty read differently from two.

Discrimination was confirmed against real files rather than constructed ones, and holding the surface fixed is what makes the comparison mean anything. Two files sat at either end: a catalog page reported 19 percent, its flattest paragraph five sentences within four words of each other and three of them verbless, while a narrative page reported none. The catalog shape is what `canon/context/cli/audits/markdown-triage.md` already records as an entry, so the measure finding it is the surface caveat firing rather than a defect it caught.

The README is the one recorded healthy sample that reads worse than the corpus, and reading a rate off it is the error rather than the file. It carries seven measured paragraphs against a corpus median drawn from files carrying ten or more, and its flattest is a three-sentence development preamble at 9, 10, and 11 words, which the measure is honestly right about. A per-file rate needs a denominator before it means anything, which is worth stating wherever this record is used to judge one file.
